# Spencer Haws: The Banker Who Built 175 Websites Before One Worked

Spencer Haws had a good job at a bank.

Business analyst. Stable. Predictable. He was, by his own description, deeply bored — the kind of bored where you start reading about other people's businesses during lunch breaks because your own work has stopped requiring your full attention.

He started building websites at night.

Not one website. Dozens.

---

## The Volume Strategy

Spencer Haws's approach was, in retrospect, absurd and also correct.

He would find a narrow topic with search traffic and low competition. He would build a small website about it — twenty or thirty articles. He would monetize it with ads or affiliate links. Then he would move to the next one.

By 2011, he had built approximately 175 websites.

Most of them made almost nothing. A few made a hundred dollars a month. A handful made more.

But the portfolio, in aggregate, made enough that in 2011 he quit the bank.

---

## What the 175 Taught Him

The websites were not the real output. The real output was pattern recognition.

Having built 175 sites, Spencer Haws had an unusually precise sense of what predicted success: which keyword patterns converted, which niches had buyer intent, which topics had monetization paths, which had none.

He also knew exactly how tedious the research was. Finding a viable niche required checking search volume, competition, domain availability, monetization options — across dozens of candidates, manually, for hours.

He built a tool to automate it, for himself.

---

## Long Tail Pro

Long Tail Pro launched in 2011 as keyword research software for people doing exactly what Spencer Haws had been doing.

He built it because he needed it. He sold it because other people needed it too.

It became a substantial software business. He eventually sold Long Tail Pro in 2017.

The pattern repeats across this book: the person who does the tedious thing at high volume eventually builds the tool that automates the tedious thing, and the tool is worth more than any individual instance of the work.

---

## Niche Pursuits

Alongside the websites and the software, Spencer Haws ran Niche Pursuits — a blog and podcast documenting everything.

Real numbers. Real projects. Real failures — and there were many, because 175 websites means at least 150 that didn't work.

The transparency built an audience of people trying to do the same thing, which became its own business through affiliate income, courses, and the sale of software to that audience.

He built the media property and the products in parallel, each feeding the other.

---

## The Later Portfolio

Spencer Haws has continued the same strategy for over a decade: build sites, test niches, publish the results, sell the tools.

He has bought and sold websites. He has run Amazon FBA experiments. He has tested every content monetization model available.

Not every experiment works. He publishes the ones that don't with the same detail as the ones that do, which is why the audience has stayed for fifteen years.

---

## The Last Thing

Spencer Haws built 175 websites before he built one that mattered.

The 175 were not failures. They were the dataset. Each one taught him something specific about what works, and the accumulated knowledge became a software company, a media business, and a career.

Most people build two websites, conclude the model doesn't work, and stop.

The difference between Spencer Haws and those people is not talent.

It is 173 more attempts.
