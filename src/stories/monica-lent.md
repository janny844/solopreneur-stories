# Monica Lent: The Engineering Manager Who Bootstrapped to $10k MRR in a Year

Monica Lent was an engineering manager in Berlin, doing well, and quietly running an experiment.

The experiment was blogging. She had built a following writing about software engineering and career development — thoughtful, technically credible posts that got read by other engineers.

She noticed something about her own writing workflow that annoyed her, and about the writing workflows of every other blogger she talked to.

Nobody could tell what was working.

---

## Affilimate

Monica Lent launched Affilimate in 2019.

The problem: content creators who made money through affiliate links had no way to see which content was actually generating revenue. Amazon's dashboard showed you sales but not which page produced them. Every affiliate network had a different interface, different reporting, different attribution.

A blogger with fifty posts and six affiliate programs had six dashboards and no unified picture.

Affilimate unified them. Connect your affiliate accounts, connect your site analytics, and see revenue attributed to specific pages, specific links, specific placements.

The insight was the same as Baremetrics: an entire category of business owners were making decisions blind because their data lived in someone else's dashboard in an unusable form.

---

## The First Year

Monica Lent built Affilimate while working, then went full-time.

She has documented the growth publicly with a level of specificity that is unusual: monthly revenue, customer counts, churn, the marketing channels that worked and the ones that didn't.

Affilimate crossed $10,000 in monthly recurring revenue within roughly a year of launch.

The customers were professional content publishers — people running affiliate businesses generating six or seven figures, for whom a tool that improved attribution paid for itself immediately.

High-value customers with an obvious ROI story. The best kind of market.

---

## Blogging Fuel

Alongside Affilimate, Monica Lent ran Blogging Fuel — a newsletter and resource for content entrepreneurs.

This was not a separate business. It was distribution, audience research, and credibility, all in one.

Writing for affiliate publishers meant constantly talking to affiliate publishers, which meant she always knew what the product needed next. The newsletter was the customer development channel.

She has written about this explicitly: the content business and the software business are not two efforts. They are one effort where the content generates both the customers and the roadmap.

---

## The Technical Founder Advantage, Used Correctly

Monica Lent was an engineering manager, which means she could build the product.

But the more relevant advantage was that she understood systems well enough to build something genuinely hard: reliable data aggregation across dozens of affiliate networks, each with different APIs, different data formats, different reporting delays, and some with no API at all.

That difficulty is the moat. A non-technical founder could not have built it. A technical founder without domain knowledge would not have known it was worth building.

She had both.

---

## The Last Thing

Monica Lent was an engineering manager in Berlin who wrote blog posts on the side and got annoyed that she couldn't tell which ones were making money.

The annoyance became a product. The blog became the distribution channel. The engineering background made the hard part possible.

Every piece of her advantage came from something she was already doing for other reasons.

That is how most of these stories actually work.

The pieces are already there. The founder is the person who notices they fit together.
