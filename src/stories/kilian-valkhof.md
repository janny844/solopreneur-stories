# Kilian Valkhof: The Developer Who Built a Browser For Developers

Kilian Valkhof was building websites and hating one specific part of it.

Not the coding. The debugging. The endless cycle of: change some CSS, switch to Chrome, check it, switch to Firefox, check it again, switch to Safari, discover it's broken, switch back to the editor, repeat.

Every front-end developer does this. Most accept it as the cost of the job.

Kilian Valkhof decided it was a product.

---

## Polypane

Polypane is a browser built for developers.

Instead of showing one viewport at a time, it shows several simultaneously — mobile, tablet, desktop, side by side, synchronized. Scroll one, they all scroll. Click one, they all click. Change your CSS, all of them update at once.

On top of that: built-in accessibility checking, color-blindness simulation, live CSS debugging, meta-tag preview, and a dozen other things a front-end developer would otherwise need six separate tools for.

He built it alone, in Electron, over years.

---

## The Hard Product

Polypane is an unusually hard product for a solo founder.

Most successful indie products are thin — a wrapper around an API, a well-designed form, a scheduling tool. They can be built in weeks and maintained in hours per month.

A browser cannot. A browser is enormous. It has to handle every website on the internet, every CSS quirk, every JavaScript framework, every operating system's rendering peculiarities. Chromium updates constantly and every update risks breaking something.

Kilian Valkhof took on that maintenance burden knowingly.

The bet: the difficulty was the moat. Nobody else was going to solo-build a developer browser, which meant if he could sustain it, he would own the category.

---

## The Pricing Decision

Polypane is not cheap. Plans run from roughly $10 to $40 per month depending on tier.

For a consumer product, this would be prohibitive. For a professional tool that saves a front-end developer thirty minutes a day, it is trivially justified. A developer billing $100/hour who saves half an hour a day saves $1,000/month.

Kilian Valkhof priced for the professional, not the hobbyist.

This is the single decision that made a solo-maintained browser financially viable. At $5/month he would have needed tens of thousands of users to sustain the maintenance burden. At $20/month he needed a fraction of that.

Fewer customers, better served, paying real money.

---

## Building In Public, Slowly

Kilian Valkhof has written extensively about the process — the technical challenges, the business decisions, the moments where the maintenance load felt unsustainable.

He does not present it as easy. He has been open about the fact that a solo developer maintaining an Electron browser is a peculiar and occasionally exhausting way to make a living.

But the product has grown steadily, year over year, funded entirely by customers. No investors. No acquisition. No pivot to something easier.

---

## The Last Thing

Kilian Valkhof was annoyed by something every front-end developer is annoyed by, every single day.

The difference between him and everyone else who was annoyed: he spent years building the fix.

Polypane exists because one person decided that a universally-tolerated inconvenience was worth solving properly, and then did the enormous unglamorous work of solving it properly.

That is most of what building a real product is.

The idea is the easy part.
