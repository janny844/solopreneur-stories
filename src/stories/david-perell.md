# David Perell: The Man Who Turned Writing Into a $5M Education Business

In 2018, David Perell made a bet.

He would write every day on the internet. He would share what he was learning about writing, about ideas, about how to think clearly. He would build an audience before he built a product.

He had no product yet. He had a newsletter, a Twitter account, and a belief that the people who most needed to learn to write — the professionals, the knowledge workers, the founders — were not being served by any existing writing education.

He turned out to be right.

---

## The Problem He Saw

David Perell grew up struggling with writing.

Not because he was unintelligent — he was clearly not unintelligent. But because writing was taught, in every school he attended, as a set of rules to follow rather than a skill to practice. Five-paragraph essays, thesis statements, topic sentences. The form without the substance.

He eventually figured out how to write by reading obsessively and imitating writers he admired. He took apart essays he loved and tried to understand why they worked. He practiced the way musicians practice — not to produce a finished product but to understand the mechanics.

By the time he was in his mid-twenties, he could write well. More importantly, he understood writing as a learnable craft, not a natural talent.

He built a course to teach it.

---

## Write of Passage

Write of Passage launched in 2019 as a six-week online course in writing for the internet.

The price was $1,000. In 2019, this was high — unusually high for an online course from someone without institutional credentials or a large book deal.

He launched to his newsletter list. The first cohort sold out.

Not because of clever marketing. Because the audience he had spent a year building trusted him. They had read his writing. They had seen him apply the principles he was teaching. They had watched him build an audience from scratch using exactly the methods he was promising to teach.

The course was the product. The newsletter was the proof of concept.

---

## The Cohort Model

Write of Passage was not a passive video course. It was a cohort — a group of students moving through the material together, workshopping each other's writing, building connections that outlasted the six weeks.

This model was unusual in online education at the time and has since become widely copied.

The cohort model produced two things that individual video courses couldn't: accountability (you're not learning alone, you're learning with a group that expects your participation) and community (the relationships formed in the cohort become part of the value, long after the content is consumed).

Alumni became evangelists. Every cohort seeded the next one through word of mouth from people who had genuinely changed how they worked.

---

## The Scale

By 2022, Write of Passage had run seventeen cohorts. Thousands of alumni. A small team of coaches and administrators.

David Perell was generating over $5 million in annual revenue from a writing education business he had built in four years from a newsletter and a Twitter account.

He had not raised money. He had not taken on a co-founder. He had written consistently, taught what he knew, and built a community around the practice.

---

## The Insight He Returns To

David Perell's central insight — the one he teaches and the one that built his business — is that ideas are the unit of competitive advantage for knowledge workers.

Not effort. Not time. Ideas.

"The person who can generate, develop, and communicate original ideas has an unfair advantage in every field," he has said. "Writing is the tool that develops that muscle."

He built a business on this insight not because it was marketable but because he believed it — because he had experienced the compounding returns of a writing practice in his own life and career.

The business is the proof of the thesis.

---

## The Last Thing

In 2018, David Perell made a bet that he would write every day on the internet.

In 2022, the daily writing had produced a newsletter, a podcast, a course business, a community, and a reputation as one of the more serious thinkers about what it means to communicate clearly.

The bet paid off.

It usually does, for people who make it long enough before stopping.
