# John Rush: The Founder Who Runs Twenty Products at Once

John Rush does something that violates every piece of startup advice ever given.

He runs many products simultaneously.

Not sequentially. Not a portfolio he acquired and delegated. Actively, concurrently, as a solo founder with minimal help: directories, AI tools, website builders, SEO products, marketplaces — twenty or more live products generating revenue at the same time.

The conventional advice is unambiguous: focus. Do one thing. Kill everything else. Every accelerator, every mentor, every book says the same thing.

He does the opposite and it works.

---

## Why Focus Is Advice For Someone Else

The focus doctrine comes from venture capital, and it makes sense in that context.

If you have raised money on the promise of a billion-dollar outcome, splitting attention across twenty small products is malpractice. You need one enormous winner and every hour spent elsewhere reduces the probability of getting it.

But John Rush is not trying to build a billion-dollar company. He is trying to maximize total revenue across a portfolio while remaining independent.

Under that objective, concentration is a bug, not a feature.

Twenty products earning $2,000/month each is $40,000/month, with no single point of failure, no dependence on any one market remaining favorable, and no scenario where a Google algorithm change or a platform policy shift destroys everything.

---

## The Systems That Make It Possible

Running twenty products alone is only possible with extreme systematization.

John Rush reuses infrastructure across products relentlessly. Shared authentication, shared billing, shared analytics, shared deployment pipelines, shared component libraries. Launching product twenty-one costs a fraction of what launching product one cost, because most of it already exists.

He also builds products that require minimal support by design. Self-serve onboarding. Documentation instead of email. Simple enough that users don't get stuck.

The constraint shapes the product selection. He does not build things that require hand-holding, because he cannot hold hands twenty times.

---

## The Marketing Machine

The other half of the system is distribution.

John Rush builds heavily in the SEO and directory space — products that acquire customers through search rather than through active selling. Once ranking, they generate signups without ongoing effort.

He also cross-promotes across his own portfolio. Each product links to the others. Each launch reaches an audience built by the previous ones. The portfolio compounds because the products feed each other traffic.

This is a structural advantage that a focused single-product founder cannot access.

---

## The Failure Rate

Not every product works. Most don't, at least not well.

This is fine, structurally, because the cost of building product twenty-one is low. A product that fails costs a few weeks of a founder who has already built the reusable infrastructure.

The math is the same as venture capital, applied at a personal scale: many small bets, most fail, the winners cover everything.

He is running a fund where he is the only portfolio company and also all of them.

---

## The Last Thing

John Rush runs twenty products at once and is told constantly that he should focus.

The advice is correct for the person giving it and wrong for him.

The lesson is not "run twenty products." The lesson is that almost all business advice is downstream of an objective function that was never stated out loud — and if your objective is different, the advice inverts.

Ask what the advisor is optimizing for.

Then ask whether it is the same thing you are.
