# Harry Dry: The Copywriter Who Studied Everyone Else's Marketing So You Don't Have To

Harry Dry launched Marketing Examples with no email list, no social following, no budget.

He had a Notion document and an idea: what if someone collected the best real-world examples of great marketing copy and explained, specifically, why each one worked?

Not principles. Not frameworks. Examples. Actual screenshots, actual words, actual reasons.

He launched in 2019. By 2023, Marketing Examples had 120,000 subscribers and was making £20,000 per month from a single, small audience.

---

## The Insight

Harry Dry is British, obsessive, and specific in a way that makes him somewhat unusual in the marketing space, where vagueness is often mistaken for depth.

His insight was simple: marketing education was full of principles and empty of evidence. Everyone taught "write good copy" and almost nobody showed you, concretely, what good copy looked like and why it worked.

He would be the person who showed you.

He spent months before launching, curating examples. Not writing theory — curating. Finding ads, landing pages, emails, tweets that had actually worked, and building a hypothesis about why.

The first issue was about the headline of a 1920s Rolls-Royce advertisement. The second was about how Dropbox's referral program copy drove viral growth. Each one: a specific example, a specific analysis, a specific takeaway.

It was not novel, exactly. But it was useful in a way that most marketing content wasn't.

---

## The Growth That Wasn't Growth

Marketing Examples grew slowly.

In the first year, Harry Dry's primary distribution was cold outreach — direct messages to marketing professionals, founders, designers who might find the content useful. One by one. Manually.

This is not a scalable strategy. It is, however, a strategy that works when you have zero audience and zero budget.

"Most people are waiting for permission to do the unscalable thing," he has said. "The unscalable thing is the only thing that works when you're starting from zero."

He sent hundreds of cold messages. Most were ignored. Some were answered. A handful of those became evangelists — people who forwarded the newsletter to their teams, who mentioned it in podcasts, who included it in their own newsletters.

One mention from a larger newsletter would add a thousand subscribers. One retweet from the right account would add five hundred.

Slow growth, punctuated by spikes. The spikes were caused by the quality. The quality was caused by the obsessive curation.

---

## What He Built (And Didn't Build)

Marketing Examples has never had:
- A team
- A podcast
- A course (until recently, and then only one)
- A community platform
- An advertising budget
- A social media strategy beyond Twitter

It has always had:
- A newsletter
- A website
- One person who reads everything about marketing and picks the best examples

The simplicity is not accidental. Harry Dry made a decision early that he would not chase breadth. He would go deep on one thing — the best marketing examples, explained clearly — and do it consistently, forever.

Every time the pressure appeared to add a YouTube channel or a community or a premium tier, he asked one question: does this make the core thing better, or does it distract from it?

Most ideas didn't survive the question.

---

## The Copy He Keeps Coming Back To

Harry Dry's own work — the newsletter itself — is a case study in the principles he teaches.

The subject lines are specific: "Why this 1970s Volkswagen ad is still the best car ad ever written" beats "Marketing tips for this week."

The headlines lead with the example, not the lesson: "How Basecamp grew to $100M ARR with one email sequence" is a story. "Email marketing best practices" is a category.

The writing is short. Each issue is readable in under five minutes. He has said that he deletes more words than he publishes — that the hardest part of the job is making the complexity small enough to fit.

"If I can't explain it simply," he says, "I don't understand it well enough to teach it."

---

## The Number

£20,000 per month from one newsletter. No team. A website, an email platform, and years of obsessive curation.

The people who subscribe to Marketing Examples subscribe because Harry Dry understood something most content creators don't: depth beats breadth, specificity beats principle, showing beats telling.

He learned this by studying a hundred years of the best marketing copy ever written.

Then he wrote that down, simply, so you could learn it in five minutes.

That's the whole thing. And it turned out to be worth quite a lot.
